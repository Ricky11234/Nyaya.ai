The uploaded legal corpus did not contain a specific list of conditions for anticipatory bail under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS). Using the external web sources, the following detailed conditions—drawn from Section 482 BNSS (the counterpart of CrPC Section 438) and the related provisions of Sections 480‑483 BNSS—can be outlined for a legal practitioner:

**Statutory Conditions expressly mentioned in Section 482(2) BNSS**  
- **Availability for police interrogation** – Applicant must make himself/herself available for questioning by a police officer whenever required. *(Source: Divorce By Law)*  
- **No inducement, threat or promise to witnesses** – Applicant shall not directly or indirectly induce, threaten or promise any person acquainted with the facts so as to dissuade that person from disclosing the facts to the Court or police. *(Source: Divorce By Law)*  
- **Restriction on foreign travel** – Applicant shall not leave India without the prior permission of the Court. *(Source: Divorce By Law)*  

**Additional conditions incorporated by reference to Section 480(3) BNSS**  
- **Attendance as per bond** – Applicant must attend court as required under the terms of the bail bond. *(Source: Lawbot Express)*  
- **No commission of a similar offence** – Applicant shall not commit any offence that is similar to the offence for which anticipatory bail is sought. *(Source: Lawbot Express)*  
- **General “other conditions” clause** – The Court may impose any other condition it deems necessary in the interests of justice, provided the condition is reasonable and tied to the purpose of bail. *(Source: Lawbot Express)*  

**Special exclusions (non‑grantable offences)**  
- Anticipatory bail **cannot be granted** for:  
  - Rape of a woman under the age of 16 years (BNS s. 65).  
  - Gang‑rape of a woman under 18 years of age (BNS s. 70(2)). *(Source: Divorce By Law)*  

**Procedural safeguards & consequences**  
- **Interim bail** – While the anticipatory bail application is pending, the applicant may seek interim bail; the same conditions (and any additional ones) will apply. *(Source: iplEaders blog)*  
- **Cancellation on breach** – If any of the above conditions are violated, the bail may be cancelled under Section 492 BNSS. *(Source: Judextutorials)*  
- **Appeal rights** – The State may challenge the anticipatory bail order in a higher court; the order can also be set aside if the Court failed to consider material facts. *(Source: Judextutorials)*  

**Typical drafting checklist for advocates**  
- State the **reasonable apprehension of arrest** for a non‑bailable offence.  
- Cite **Section 482(1) BNSS** (requirement of apprehension & non‑bailable nature).  
- List the **statutory conditions** (availability for interrogation, no witness tampering, travel restriction).  
- Mention any **additional conditions** the Court may impose under **Section 480(3) BNSS**.  
- Include a **bond/surety** clause as per standard bail practice.  
- Highlight the **exclusions** (rape of minors) to pre‑empt objections. *(Source: iplEaders blog)*  

**Key take‑aways for practice**  
- The core conditions are **uniform across all courts** (Magistrate, Sessions, High Court) because BNSS consolidates the CrPC provisions.  
- Conditions must be **reasonable, proportionate, and directly linked** to preventing flight, tampering with evidence, or repeat offences—per Supreme Court jurisprudence (Sumit Mehta, Munish Bhasin, etc., now embodied in BNSS).  
- Courts retain **discretionary power** to tailor conditions to the facts; however, overly punitive or irrelevant conditions may be struck down.
