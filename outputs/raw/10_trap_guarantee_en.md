**What the law says (in plain language)**  

- You can sue your neighbour for the damage to your car if you can show that he was **negligent** in caring for the tree (for example, he ignored a legal requirement to keep the tree trimmed or to maintain a safe distance from the road).  
- The neighbour may try to claim the fall was an **“act of God”** (a storm).  Courts sometimes accept that defence if the tree was healthy and the neighbour did nothing wrong.  If the tree was old, diseased, or the neighbour had been warned to cut it, the defence is weaker.  
- **Section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act** is sometimes used for accidents involving vehicles, but the external material says it may not apply when the vehicle is not moving and the tree is the main cause.  So a claim under that section might not be the right route.  
- A regular **tort claim for negligence** (under the Indian law of torts) is the usual way to recover property damage.  You would need to prove: (1) the neighbour owed a duty to keep the tree safe, (2) he breached that duty, (3) the breach caused the tree to fall, and (4) you suffered Rs 5 lakh loss.  
- If you win, the court can order the neighbour to pay the amount you claim, plus interest and possibly legal costs.  There is no specific monetary ceiling for a tort claim, so Rs 5 lakh is within what a court can award.  
- The claim must be filed within the **limitation period** for torts, which is generally three years from the date you discovered the damage (or should have discovered it).  Missing this deadline can bar the claim.  

**Quick checklist**  

- Gather evidence: photos of the fallen tree, any notice you gave the neighbour about the tree, maintenance records, and a repair estimate for the car.  
- Check if the neighbour had any official notice or order to trim the tree (e.g., from the municipal authority).  
- File a civil suit for negligence within three years of the accident.  
- Be ready for the neighbour to argue “act of God”; you’ll need proof of his neglect to counter that.
