The notice you got is a legal demand that says you owe Rs 2.3 lakh on a credit‑card you say you never applied for. It tells you to reply within 15 days or the bank will start legal action.  

**Important:** This is a court‑related paper with a deadline. Do not ignore it. Show the notice to a lawyer before the 15‑day deadline. You can get free legal help by calling NALSA at 15100. I can help you understand the notice, but a qualified lawyer must check any reply you send.  

**What the notice means and what you can do**

- **Check if the notice was correctly addressed.**  
  The Supreme Court has said that a demand notice must be properly addressed for the “pay within 15 days” rule to apply (Section 27 of the General Clauses Act). If the address on the notice does not match the address the bank has on record, the notice may be defective and the 15‑day payment requirement may not apply.

- **Ask the bank for a full explanation in writing.**  
  RBI rules require the card‑issuing bank to give a complete statement of the amount claimed and any supporting documents within 30 days of a complaint about a wrong bill. Request this under the RBI’s “fair practices in debt collection” guidelines.

- **Raise a formal dispute with the bank.**  
  Send a written complaint (registered post or email) stating that you never applied for the card, that you dispute the debt, and that you want the bank to cancel the card and remove any charges. Keep a copy of everything.

- **If the bank does not resolve it, go to the Banking Ombudsman.**  
  You can approach the Banking Ombudsman within 30 days of lodging your complaint with the bank. The Ombudsman can order the bank to correct the mistake and may award compensation for harassment.

- **Consider filing a consumer complaint online.**  
  Use the e‑Jagriti portal (e‑jagriti.gov.in) to file a consumer complaint against the bank. The appropriate consumer commission depends on the amount: District Commission (up to Rs 50 lakh), State Commission (Rs 50 lakh‑Rs 2 crore), National Commission (above Rs 2 crore).

- **Keep records and protect yourself.**  
  Save the notice, your complaint letters, any replies, and bank statements. Do not make any payment until the dispute is resolved or you have legal advice.

**Next steps**

1. Verify the address on the notice against the bank’s records.  
2. Send a written dispute to the bank asking for a detailed statement and cancellation of the card.  
3. If the bank’s reply is unsatisfactory, file a complaint with the Banking Ombudsman and/or on e‑Jagriti.  
4. Consult a lawyer before the 15‑day deadline to draft your reply.
