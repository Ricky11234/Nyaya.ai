### How anticipatory bail works under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 (BNSS)

| Point | What the law says (source) |
|-------|----------------------------|
| **Who can apply** | A person can seek anticipatory bail by filing an application under **Section 482 BNSS** in the High Court or the Court of Session (as shown in case practice). |
| **When the court may grant bail** | **Section 485(1) BNSS** allows a High Court or Court of Session to direct that a person in custody be released on bail. |
| **Conditions the court may impose** | • **Section 480(3) BNSS** – for offences punishable with imprisonment of 7 years or more (or offences listed in Chapters VI, VII, XVII of the BNS), the court must impose the following conditions: <br> 1. Appear before the court when required (as per the bail bond). <br> 2. Not commit any offence of a similar nature while bail is in force. <br> 3. Not tamper with, conceal or destroy evidence, nor induce any witness to withhold testimony. <br>• **Section 485(1) BNSS** – the court may also impose any other condition it considers necessary for the purposes mentioned in sub‑section (3) of Section 482. |
| **When the mandatory conditions apply** | The mandatory conditions of Section 480(3) apply only if the offence is punishable with imprisonment of **seven years or more**, or if it falls under **Chapters VI, VII, or XVII** of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 (LiveLaw article). They are **not** mandatory for all offences. |

### Practical steps a lawyer would take

1. **Draft the anticipatory bail petition** under **Section 482 BNSS**, stating why the applicant believes arrest is likely and describing the nature of the alleged offence.  
2. **Cite the relevant provisions** – Section 482 for the right to apply, Section 485(1) for the court’s power to release on bail, and Section 480(3) for the mandatory conditions (if they apply).  
3. **Argue that the applicant will comply** with any conditions the court may impose (e.g., appearing when required, not tampering with evidence).  
4. **Request that the court impose only reasonable conditions**, pointing out that the mandatory conditions of Section 480(3) are limited to offences with imprisonment of 7 years or more.  
5. **Be prepared to address any objections** from the prosecution, such as concerns about evidence tampering or witness intimidation, by showing the applicant’s willingness to cooperate with the investigation.
